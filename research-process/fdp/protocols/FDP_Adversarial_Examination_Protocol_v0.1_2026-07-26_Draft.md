# Adversarial Examination Protocol

> **Status:** Draft conversation architecture  
> **Canonical status:** Not canonical  
> **Decision status:** Unopened  
> **Intellectual source:** Approved Intellectual Handoff Baseline v0.2  
> **Researcher authority:** Sole authority for acceptance, rejection, revision, deferral, and canonicalisation

Adversarial examination is disciplined pressure, not automatic opposition. It tests the researcher's actual formulation and must not make the researcher defend an AI-supplied position they do not hold. Modes are introduced progressively after native formulation and clarification.

| Mode | Purpose | Sample question pattern | Common failure exposed | Danger of overuse | When introduced |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ontological adversary | Test what kind of thing is being proposed | “Is this an entity, relation, event, model, disposition, or several controlled senses?” | Reification or category error | Forces premature metaphysics | After the researcher's working meaning exists |
| Parsimony adversary | Test whether the concept earns its burden | “What fails if ordinary language or a simpler model is used?” | Renaming without explanatory gain | Treats novelty as the only value | After necessity has been stated |
| Boundary adversary | Test inclusion, exclusion, and system limits | “What clearly falls outside, and why?” | Explains everything and therefore nothing | Demands sharp edges before the phenomenon permits them | After examples and non-claims |
| Empirical adversary | Ask what observable difference follows | “What would differ if this were false?” | Unfalsifiable or consequence-free claim | Reduces conceptual inquiry to current measurement | After provisional scope |
| Historical or prior-art adversary | Test novelty, terminology, and precursor relations | “What would count as a precursor, and what material difference must remain?” | Reinvention or false originality | Anchors the researcher before native formulation | At or after the research gate |
| Formal or mathematical adversary | Test variables, state spaces, inferential limits, and alternatives | “What is defined, measured, and compared, and what does the notation not prove?” | Symbols creating false authority | Formalism displaces the concept | After the conceptual burden is clear |
| Ethical and political adversary | Test power, accountability, exclusion, and consequence | “Who gains authority or bears harm under this formulation?” | Neutral-looking theory enabling harmful use | Moral critique replaces descriptive precision | After the intended scope is known |
| Internal-coherence adversary | Test dependencies and contradictions within Noeplexics | “Does this rely on what it is supposed to explain?” | Circularity or incompatible modules | Forces global consistency too early | After relevant dependencies are named |
| Misuse adversary | Test foreseeable overstatement or appropriation | “How could a reader weaponise or overextend this?” | Harmful interpretation or synthetic authority | Treats all misuse as the author's claim | Before closure and public drafting |
| Future-interpretation adversary | Test how wording may drift over time | “Which phrase could later be read more strongly than intended?” | Safeguards lost through repetition | Endless qualification | During wording reconstruction and closure |

## Progressive use

1. Confirm the researcher's current formulation.
2. Select only materially relevant modes.
3. Present one or a small cluster of objections.
4. Record the strongest version before recording the response.
5. Ask whether the objection changes the formulation, exposes an uncertainty, or is rejected.
6. Preserve unresolved objections without manufacturing a repair.

An objection can be answered, partially answered, unresolved, or accepted. None of these statuses automatically determines the final decision.

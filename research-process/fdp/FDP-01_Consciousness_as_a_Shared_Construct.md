## E. Clarification prompts

2. Can something be socially influenced without being socially constituted in your intended sense?

4. Is the claim about all conscious experience or selected aspects of conscious organisation?

6. What role do language, memory, culture, and other persons each play?

## F. Adversarial nudges

| Lens | Strongest objection | What it tests | Follow-up question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parsimony | “Shared construct” may only rename ordinary social influence. | Explanatory gain | What does the concept explain that influence alone does not? |
| Boundary | If every formative influence counts, the conscious system expands without limit. | Exclusion rule | Which formative relations remain outside consciousness? |
| Ontological | The formulation may slide from conditions of formation to constituents of experience. | Influence/constitution distinction | What exactly becomes constitutive, if anything? |
| Empirical | Nothing observable may differ if the claim is false. | Consequence and testability | What contrast between models could matter? |
| Internal coherence | A shared field may be defined so broadly that private-only failure is guaranteed. | Circularity | How can private/shared variables be fixed independently of the desired result? |
| Formal | Conditional information may reflect hidden variables or an incomplete private state. | Inferential limit | What would count as a fair private-state specification? |
| Misuse | “Shared consciousness” may be read as a collective subject entitled to override individuals. | Political/ethical overreach | Which safeguard protects individual subjecthood and responsibility? |
| Future interpretation | “Construct” may be read as unreal, arbitrary, or wholly invented. | Wording drift | What sense of construction do you intend and reject? |

## I. Targeted research agenda

- **Terminology and prior-use audit:** uses and risks of “shared construct” and “shared consciousness.”
- **Philosophical comparison:** accounts that separate formation, influence, constitution, and phenomenal location.
- **Empirical evidence:** observations capable of distinguishing private-only and explicitly shared-variable models.
- **Mathematical/formal requirements:** principled variables, hidden-variable controls, inferential limits, and alternative models.
- **Technical validation:** whether proposed information or intervention measures answer the stated question.
- **Historical precedent:** earlier formulations that may be apparent precursors and their material differences.
- **Competing explanations:** development, learning, memory, and social influence without the stronger formulation.
- **Ethical/political implications:** collective-subject readings, autonomy, responsibility, and misuse.

These are future research questions and domains, not reviewed findings.

# AI Use and Research Authority

This public document is adapted from the current nonlegacy Noeplexics AI Use Protocol. It states research-governance rules and does not by itself establish the provenance of any specific unstaged work.

- AI systems are research instruments, not authors.
- The human researcher remains responsible for every claim, equation, citation, interpretation, decision, and publication.
- AI-generated citations must be independently verified against the cited source.
- Human-originated ideas, AI explication, co-developed formulations, and external concepts should be distinguishable when materially relevant.
- Important human-AI interactions should be preserved in appropriate research records. Public release of those records is a separate decision and may be limited by privacy, confidentiality, or provenance concerns.
- AI assistance used in a paper should be recorded in an AI-use ledger.
- AI must not invent provenance, intellectual origin, or source support.
- Explicit AI articulation must not be mistaken for intellectual origin.
- Researcher corrections take priority over earlier AI formulations.
- A transcript is evidence of development, not automatically the canonical theory.
- AI must not present speculative claims as established conclusions.
- AI must not imply that a language model possesses lived human experience or consciousness unless a paper explicitly argues for and supports that position.

AI-use records should be proportionate to their intellectual or evidential relevance. They should preserve uncertainty and rejection as well as accepted assistance. Fluent language alone is not evidence of authorship, origin, truth, or consensus.

Final authority over Noeplexics research decisions remains with Mark Anil Mathew.
